# Overview
This dashboard is intended to inform the user about recyclable materials and give a mini crash course on the waste management system. It allows them to play with a few different figures comparing the way different materials have been handled over the years 1960-2018. There is a lot more information on the dashboard so I won't repeat it, but there are so many ways to customize the data and make comparisons, whether its between materials, management methods, or over time. The dashboard has an overview tab and an action step tab that provide context for the visuals and have links to external resources.

# The Data
The datasets used in this dashboard are from the US Environmental Protection Agency, and were acquired from data.gov. They are the first four pages from a large file, seperated into their own CSVs for our purposes.  

# Visualization Strategoes
For this dashboard I wanted to make a project that feels complex yet simple, and complete. The overview and action step tabs are intentionally placed at the start and end and aim to raise questions that the viewer can take into the interactive tabs or out into their lives when they are done. There is a consistent blue and white color palette, reminiscent of the recycling bins themselves. There are many dropdowns that impact the visuals, but they are all clearly labeled and come with default options meant to ease the user in to the experience. The line graph is simple enough, but I put extra work into making the horizontal bars from the second visual readable. The sub-bars are all labeled with the percentage they represent, and colored to connect to their material fate. The paragraphs which lie underneath the visuals encourage the users to try a few things out that will lead to discovery. I had a lot of fun putting this dashboard together and polishing it up, even if it wasn't all ready by the time I had to present.
